## Electrical continuity tests

On 2026-06-26, Jordan, Rashi, and Phil did some continuity tests on the partially-constructed horn with a multimeter. These were motivated by poor performance of a probe in the waveguide earlier in the week. The weatehr was dry, sunny, and very hot (above 30 deg C), and so there should not have been much moisture present.

The multimeter was relatively inaccurate, giving readings around 10-20 Ohms when shorting the probes (connected via crocodile clips). Still, it was clear when there was electrical continuity. Connecting the probes to pairs of metal bolts that hold together the mesh panels, low resistance was seen, showing that there is a good conductive path through the structure. This the case even for bolts around the corner of the waveguide or several panels away. More accurate resistance readings with a better multimeter would be useful to assess loss.

Connecting the probes to the mesh was more difficult because of a surface layer of rust. Attaching a crocodile clip directly often resulting in no connection, so we had to scrape away the rust first. After doing this, a good connection could be obtained, although the resistance readings seemed higher, e.g. around 50 Ohms. We did tests of continuity on either side of the waveguide from within the waveguide, and also around the service door, finding generally good continuity all thebway around. We did try a point higher in the waveguide too, but were not able to reach the flare section. A more systematic approach with a better multimeter would be desirable.

The rust probably contributed to the poor performance of the probe earlier in the week. A suggested improvement is to use brass split bolts (found in earthing kits) to attach the base plate of the probe to the waveguide, after scraping away some of the rust. This will hopefully offer a good contact with good weatherproofing. Similar could be used to hold the service door closed.
